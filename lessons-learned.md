# Lessons Learned

In addition to the technical requirements above, CS 553 students must submit a short lessons-learned write-up.

Imagine that you have been asked to explain what you learned during this project to one of the following audiences:

- Undergraduate computer science students who are about to build their first authenticated API.
- A technical manager or CEO who needs to understand the important design decisions, risks, and lessons from the project.

Choose one audience and write approximately 500–750 words explaining the most important lessons you learned while building the project.

Your write-up should address:

- The most important technical concepts you learned.
- Why database design, authentication, and authorization must be considered together.
- At least one design decision you made and the tradeoffs involved.
- At least one problem, mistake, or unexpected difficulty you encountered.
- What you would do differently if you began the project again.
- The advice you would give someone preparing to build a similar system.
- At least one security risk that a development team should not overlook.

This should not be a chronological list of tasks you completed.  Treat it as a short teaching document or lessons-learned presentation in written form.  Focus on the conclusions and practical advice another student, developer, or decision-maker could use.

You may include small code examples, diagrams, or tables, but they are not required.
